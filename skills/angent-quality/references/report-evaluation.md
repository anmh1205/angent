# Report and response argument evaluation

Use when conclusions have material consequences or an authoring-method revision risks propagation. Do not gate routine short chat on a formal ceremony.

## Check the whole argument

- Outcome and scope: does the lead reflect the verified state and inspected boundary?
- Claim support: does the evidence directly establish each decisive claim, or is inference labeled?
- Warrant: can a reader follow why the evidence supports the claim and why a competing account is weaker?
- Impact: are priority, severity and operational consequences observed, inferred with conditions or unknown?
- Action fit: does each recommendation address the causal mechanism or important uncertainty?
- Structure: do middle sections earn the conclusion, with paragraph continuity and depth intact through late sections?
- Publication: do final destination blocks preserve diagrams, citations, code and the established inference?

A missing domain premise belongs to Research/Reasoning, poor expression to Authoring, destination loss to Format and ambiguous effects to Execution. An elegant executive summary cannot compensate for ungrounded middle sections.

## Calibration cases

Compare outputs from activated baseline and candidate, matched for model, host, evidence access and task constraints when feasible. Include a one-line negative control, a conceptual misunderstanding follow-up, a cross-layer technical explanation, an audit with explicit scope, a research synthesis with disagreement, an operational status with pending effects, an early/middle/late long-form sample and a rendered chat-to-document transfer. Evaluate concrete relationships and claims, not the number of headings or token count. Human review and demonstrated reader understanding are meaningful evidence; model self-scoring is not sufficient.
